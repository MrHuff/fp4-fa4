# SM100 D64 causal GQA backward tuning

Date: 2026-08-14

Device: GPU 0

Geometry unless noted: B=1, Hq=32, Hkv=8, D=64, causal

Timing: warm cache, CuTe event timing

## Retained configuration

- Q stages: 2
- dO stages: 2 for BF16, 1 for FP8
- dK/dV stages: 2
- reduction/compute registers: 136/136
- MMA/load registers: 96/96
- split query-head ownership for GQA
- BF16 dK/dV partials, FP32 merge, vector width 4, 128 threads
- merge fused into the 128-thread dQ conversion grid at S>=1024
- 16-row fused conversion tile; retain the separate merge at S512

The original control uses one CTA per `(KV head, K tile)` and loops over all
four associated query heads.  The retained path launches one CTA per
`(query head, K tile)`, then merges the four dK/dV partials.  dQ already has
per-query-head ownership and needs no additional merge.

## Sequence sweep

| Sequence | Serialized GQA (us) | Separate merge (us) | Retained dispatch (us) | Speedup | Reduction |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 512 | 71.690 | 60.262 | 60.262 | 1.190x | 15.94% |
| 1024 | 114.777 | 83.558 | 78.519 | 1.462x | 31.59% |
| 2048 | 202.033 | 173.636 | 166.106 | 1.216x | 17.78% |
| 4096 | 521.544 | 453.207 | 440.730 | 1.183x | 15.50% |
| 8192 | 1483.264 | 1372.416 | 1351.509 | 1.097x | 8.88% |

The split benefit shrinks with sequence length because the serialized K-tile
grid eventually supplies enough CTAs to fill the GPU.  Fusing the merge into
dQ conversion returns another 1.5--6.0% from S1024 upward, but is 5.2% slower
at S512; the default therefore keeps the standalone merge only at S512.

## Head-count sensitivity at S4096, ratio 4

| Hq / Hkv | Serialized (us) | Split (us) | Speedup | Latency reduction |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 16 / 4 | 349.785 | 255.015 | 1.372x | 27.09% |
| 32 / 8 | 521.544 | 453.207 | 1.151x | 13.10% |
| 64 / 16 | 863.369 | 844.527 | 1.022x | 2.18% |

This confirms that the gain is primarily CTA-level parallelism.  At Hq=64 the
serialized grid is already broad, so the merge nearly cancels the scheduling
benefit.

## Profile attribution at S4096, Hq32/Hkv8

Nsight Systems averages for the retained split kernel:

| Kernel | Time (us) |
| --- | ---: |
| Main backward | 377.462 |
| dK/dV vector merge | 18.082 |
| dQ conversion | 30.366 |
| O*dO / scaled-LSE pass | 26.002 |

The first scalar merge took about 31.1 us.  Packed partial publication reduced
the complete path from 674 us to 468 us, and vectorizing the merge reduced it
again to 453 us.  Wider 256-bit vectors and 256-thread blocks regressed; the
retained vector-4/128-thread launch was best.

The corresponding tuned serialized profile had a roughly 464.6 us main
kernel, 29.2 us dQ conversion, and 25.5 us O*dO pass.  Splitting therefore
removes about 87 us from the main kernel and returns 18 us as an explicit
merge.  Folding that merge into the conversion grid lowers the retained result
to 440.730 us.  This is 1.183x faster than the tuned serialized control, 1.384x
faster than the original roughly 610 us control, and only 1.61% slower than the
433.73 us equivalent MHA control.

The fused generic FP8-input/BF16-output path measures 463.155 us at S4096,

## Selective packed-ALU EX2 (2026-08-14 follow-up)

The D64 FP8 profile did not contain ordinary local-memory spills.  A fresh
GPU-0 source-counter pair found zero FP8 register-spill instructions (BF16
actually executed 48,608) and attributed the FP8 loss to latency: FP8 executed
2.96% more instructions, had 2.31x the BF16 short-scoreboard samples, and had
39% more barrier samples.  Both modes ended at the same dQ reduction wait.

The retained experiment ports the forward kernel's packed-ALU EX2 range
reduction into the CuTe control without modifying the NVIDIA source.  Native
EX2 is retained for most score pairs; a compile-time cadence replaces selected
pairs with either a degree-2 or degree-1 polynomial.  Replacing every pair is
slower.  Selective replacement breaks the serialized MUFU cadence and provides
useful instruction-level parallelism.

Clean GPU-0 timings (warm cache, all modes compiled independently):

| S | BF16 (us) | Native FP8 (us) | Safe FP8: degree-2/period-2 (us) | Aggressive FP8: degree-1/period-3 (us) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 512 | 58.577 | 59.853 | 60.506 | 64.397 |
| 1024 | 78.911 | 79.241 | 77.827 | 77.375 |
| 2048 | 165.998 | 170.454 | 167.365 | 165.008 |
| 4096 | 441.114 | 463.006 | 449.459 | 442.277 |
| 8192 | 1351.064 | 1455.821 | rejected | 1377.100 |

The training-oriented aggressive candidate should therefore be dispatched only
from S1024 upward after a convergence gate.  It is 1.95% and 0.60% faster than BF16 at S1024 and S2048,
within 0.26% at S4096, and narrows the S8192 FP8 deficit from 7.74% to 1.93%.
At S4096 it improves native FP8 by 4.48%; at S8192 the improvement is 5.41%.
S512 remains a BF16 dispatch.  Degree-1/period-2 is the speed ceiling measured
so far: 440.390 us at S4096 (0.16% faster than BF16) and 1368.678 us at S8192,
but it has a weaker accuracy trade-off.

Small-shape reference checks quantify that trade-off:

| EX2 rung | dQ rel-L2 / cosine | dK rel-L2 / cosine | dV rel-L2 / cosine |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Native FP8 | 0.0141 / 0.999903 | 0.0165 / 0.999864 | 0.2521 / 0.970533 |
| Degree-2, period 2 | 0.0141 / 0.999902 | 0.0165 / 0.999864 | 0.2521 / 0.970502 |
| Degree-1, period 3 | 0.0418 / 0.999370 | 0.0377 / 0.999496 | 0.2577 / 0.970561 |
| Degree-1, period 2 | 0.0526 / 0.999255 | 0.0495 / 0.999389 | 0.2603 / 0.970601 |

The degree-2 rung is effectively lossless relative to the existing FP8 error.
The period-3 degree-1 rung keeps every cosine above the already-limiting dV
cosine while buying most of the speed; it should still be convergence-gated
before becoming the training default.

At S4096, Nsight Compute measures the aggressive main kernel at 384.416 us
versus 402.400 us for native FP8, a 4.47% reduction.  Long-scoreboard samples
fall 5791 -> 5209, wait samples 1857 -> 1463, and MIO-throttle samples
1544 -> 1215.  The kernel remains at 128 static registers with zero spills.
The next exposed hotspot is the dQ TMEM-read/reduction handoff; closing the
remaining long-sequence gap needs a structural dQ ownership change rather than
more EX2 replacement.

Safety gates were added for invalid dynamic-register allocations.  D64 compute
allocations above 144, reduction allocations below 128, and total warp-group
budgets above 512 are rejected before compilation.  A one-stage D64 Q pipeline
is also rejected because it deadlocks the SM100 mainloop.  The safe register
frontier retained 136/136 reduction/compute registers; 128/144 tied it, while
144/136 regressed.

## Rejected ceiling experiments

Keeping P in a compact BF16 register fragment expanded the main kernel by 134
SASS instructions and regressed 25.9%.  Reusing quantized FP8 P, changing the
dQ TMA publication from one to three stages, deeper Q/dO/dK-dV pipelines, and
alternate EX2 masks did not beat the retained schedule.  Degree-2/period-2 is
pathological at S8192 and is not a valid long-sequence dispatch.

The remaining GQA tax is about 7 us relative to MHA.  Packed BF16x2 atomic
publication preserved cosine above 0.999995 but regressed S4096 to 487.076 us:
four-way contention is more expensive than the fused scratch reduction.  A
packed FP32x2 reducer also stayed within noise/slightly regressed because its
extra register-fragment movement offset the lower add count.  A direct
TMA-reduce epilogue remains structurally possible, but the maximum remaining
wall-time opportunity is now only 1.61% at this shape.
